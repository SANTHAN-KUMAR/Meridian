# CLOSURE — moe-phone, 2026-09-19: can the OnePlus 15R run unmodified Qwen3-30B-A3B at 10 tok/s?

**Verdict. No — not at the phone's sustained clock with a negligible quality loss.** Every route to the target was tested by a
pre-registered decisive experiment (research spec v2, `2026-09-19_RESEARCH_SPEC.md`, closing rule §0), and each one closed.
The best lossless configuration is bounded at **~7.8 tok/s**. The research closes here per the user's rule. Every number below carries
its artifact.

## 1. The routes and what closed each

| route | decisive result | artifact |
|---|---|---|
| **CPU, lossless** (llama.cpp's repacked i8mm kernels for every weight, pre-repacked experts on flash) | pure compute floor **71.0 ms/token** throttled (generic 93.7; the repacked gain of 20.7 ms is the largest lossless compute win measured). With the measured I/O (stall 34.6 + cache management 22.0 ms), **127.6 ms -> 7.8 tok/s at best**. 10 tok/s would need compute <= 43.4 ms | `results/2026-09-19/E1_VERDICT.md` |
| **GPU as a helper** (bit-exact or tolerant Adreno kernel for ~2 experts/layer) | at the engine's dispatch size the fixed ~0.3 ms round trip alone exceeds the CPU's whole cost. The tolerant v5 kernel: k=2 **1.47 ms** host (kill >= 0.40) | `results/2026-09-19/E4_VERDICT.md` |
| **GPU as the main engine** (whole graph resident on the Adreno, llama.cpp OpenCL) | 8 Qwen3 layers take 51.5 ms/token sustained (CPU: 17.5); the full model is >= ~99 ms by monotonicity + bandwidth bounds (kill > 50.5) | `results/2026-09-19/E7_VERDICT.md` |
| **Tier A: lossy routing** (top-7/6, route-ahead, drop-cold, substitute) | against a Q8_0 reference, **none is negligible** (<= 10% more error than Q4_0 itself). The options with real speed potential add 23-78% more error than the 4-bit file; drop-cold 0.5 nearly passes but is worth ~5 ms | `results/2026-09-19/bmoe_e6/README.md` |
| memory/cache relocation (slot arena, kgsl pinning, swap guard) | net-neutral: the cost moves between terms (e.g. mgmt -15 ms, compute +12, stall +4) | `research/2026-09-19_STATE_OF_RESEARCH.md` §4 |
| speculative decoding, NPU, GPU tier (earlier) | closed with measured mechanisms | same, §4 |

**The measurement is sound:** the phone build is deterministic (Q4_0 against itself: KL 0.000000, 0/3,258 flips); the unplugged runs
were held Awake after the autosuspend confound was found; every campaign was pre-registered before its first row.

## 2. The one conditional path (unmeasured, stated honestly)
Compute scales with the CPU clock, and the phone sustains ~1.8/1.65 GHz against 3.32/3.80 hardware because the clock cap tracks skin
temperature. At full clock the repacked compute plausibly falls to ~36-44 ms, and then 10 tok/s lossless is at the edge
(~93-101 ms/token). **This is a projection, not a measurement.** E1's full-clock rows could not reach their precondition: the
phone stayed at 34.6 C while held awake. It would need sustained external cooling, which the user excluded as a deliverable.

## 3. What the project did achieve (the paper material; framings in `2026-09-19_paper_framing.md`)
1. **Lossless speed over the published baseline on the same phone:** 6.76 tok/s measured with the stack (awake, unplugged) against
   BigMoeOnEdge's reproduced 3.98 tok/s, plus the measured −20.7 ms/token repacked-kernel compute cut (projected ~7.8 tok/s).
   *Two measurements are still needed to make this a defensible headline* (§4).
2. **The first measured quality frontier for training-free MoE routing shortcuts on a phone** (E6: KL against a Q8_0 reference,
   per-token, with a determinism control).
3. **A benchmark-validity finding:** unplugged Android phones autosuspend mid-run unless held awake, so rows become bimodal. It
   invalidated two of our own campaigns before it was found; ordinary on-device benchmarks are exposed to it.
4. **A negative-results catalogue with measured mechanisms:** GPU helper and GPU-main, NPU, speculative decoding with a draft,
   memory pinning, relocation levers, lossy routing.
5. **Engineering that transfers to other devices** (the user's product goal): on-device repack conversion (`host/repack_bench/repack_gguf.cpp`,
   marker-guarded), the replay mode for measuring compute floors (`--fixed-routing`), the KL fidelity tool (`--ppl-dump/--ppl-ref`),
   the thermal/awake-controlled campaign harness.

## 4. Left out, and needed only for the paper (not for the 10 tok/s question), ~2 phone-hours
1. The end-to-end A/B of the repacked engine on real routing (`device/bmoe_prerepack.sh`, with its fidelity gate). E1 measured the
   compute cut under forced routing; the headline needs it on normal decoding.
2. The same-session head-to-head against BigMoeOnEdge's published engine (`device/bmoe_h2h.sh`, written, never run).
3. Optional: the full-clock measurement under external cooling (§2), and the instruction counters (use `cpu-cycles:u`).

## 5. What went wrong in the method, so it is not repeated
Summarised from `2026-09-19_STATE_OF_RESEARCH.md` §5 and the spec §2 and §9:
- effort went to levers with known small ceilings;
- the largest measured gap (generic vs repacked kernels) waited ~30 hours;
- relocation levers were A/B'd although the system was at a resource limit;
- proxies were used without transfer tests;
- campaigns were sized below their resolution.
The spec's floor-first, wall-time-only, remove-don't-move rules came out of these. Tonight's own slips were caught and recorded:
- a stale chain list;
- a scorer that trusted an impossible fit;
- a wrong simpleperf event name;
- a mechanism stated as fact.
