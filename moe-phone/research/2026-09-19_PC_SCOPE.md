# Scope beyond the phone: PCs and "any device" (2026-09-19)

Question from the user: does the architecture (flash-streamed MoE experts, a RAM cache, compute on the CPU and/or GPU) extend to any
device, PCs included? Measured facts are marked **[M]**, arithmetic on them **[T]**, estimates **[E]**. Estimates become claims only
through the per-cell protocol of `2026-09-19_PAPER_PLAN.md` §4.

## 1. What this laptop measures [M]
- CPU i7-14650HX (8P+8E cores, AVX2 + AVX-VNNI, no AVX-512/AMX); 16 GB RAM (~5 GB free during the probe); RTX 4060 Laptop, 8 GB VRAM,
  PCIe 4.0 x8; Samsung PM9A1-class NVMe.
- **Expert-slice random reads** (`host/pc_probe/nvme_expert_reads.py`, 0.88 MB O_DIRECT reads = one Qwen3 gate/up slice;
  `results/2026-09-19/pc_probe/nvme_expert_reads.txt`):

| filesystem | 1 lane | 4 lanes | 8 lanes | 16 lanes | p50 latency at 8 lanes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| native btrfs | 1.17 GB/s | 4.32 | **6.19** | 6.42 | 1.1 ms |
| NTFS through FUSE (ntfs-3g, the "New Volume" data partition) | 0.67 | 0.47 | 0.44 | 0.44 | 15.5 ms |

  **The filesystem decides whether the engine works on a PC.** The same drive is 14x slower through FUSE and degrades with
  concurrency. A product must detect this (fstype `fuseblk`) and store models on a native filesystem. (Every laptop run in this repo
  streamed from the FUSE partition. They were plumbing checks only, never speed claims.)
- OLMoE-1B-7B with the replay (`--fixed-routing`, 4 threads, x86 generic kernels): 20 ms/token for ~0.73 GB of active weights, i.e.
  ~36 GB/s of weight throughput at 4 threads (`results/2026-09-19/e1_laptop/fixed.out`).

## 2. The same budget equation on any device [T]
tok/s ≈ 1000 / (compute_ms + stall_ms + mgmt_ms), with
- compute_ms ≈ active_bytes_per_token / kernel_throughput (the E1 floor method; the GPU/NPU share only if it passes E4/E7-style tests);
- stall_ms ≈ (1 − hit) × expert_bytes_per_token / storage_random_read_rate, with hit given by the cache/model ratio and the routing
  locality (the project's simulator, `engine_sim_*`);
- mgmt_ms: measured per engine (~20 ms on the phone; not yet measured on a PC).
So the device probes that matter are: the storage random-read rate at the expert-slice size, the CPU (and GPU) kernel rate on the model's
shapes, the free RAM (and VRAM), and the sustained-clock behaviour. That set is exactly the product's hardware detection.

## 4. Scope verdict
- **The architecture transfers.** The same three terms govern a PC. NVMe is ~2x the phone's flash, the CPU is faster and not skin-limited,
  and the GPU has its own VRAM (so the PCIe-copy economics differ from the phone's unified memory, where E4/E7 closed the GPU).
- **The niche differs by device class.** On phones every large MoE exceeds RAM, so streaming is always the enabler. On PCs the enabler
  is models **larger than RAM (+VRAM)**: 80B-120B-class MoEs on 16-32 GB machines. Below that, existing tools compete directly and
  the novelty is thin.
- **The product's core is the performance model.** Probe the device (storage random-read rate at slice size, kernel GB/s, free RAM/VRAM,
  filesystem, sustained clock), predict tok/s for each candidate model from its active and total bytes and its routing locality, and
  recommend. The FUSE finding shows why the probes must be measured, not read from spec sheets.
- Next measurement (paper plan): on this laptop, gpt-oss-20b and one model above RAM (Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B), with the models on the native
  filesystem, CPU-only and with the GPU. Each gets a pre-registered prediction first.
