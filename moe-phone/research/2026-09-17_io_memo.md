# Removing the residual I/O stall and cache-management cost in BigMoeOnEdge, losslessly — research memo (2026-09-17)

*AI research subagent output (Claude Opus, web search). Provenance [U] per `research/README.md`;
the memo's own "not verified" marks are kept. Reproduced verbatim except for formatting.*

Validity level: this is an L3/L5 scoping memo (what variation exists to exploit, and what the
arithmetic permits) — no estimates are reported, only bounds and evidence classes.

## Shared arithmetic (used throughout)

From the given measurements: 48 layers × 8 experts = 384 expert-events/token; 22% miss = **84.5
expert misses/token**; at 2.65 MB each that is 213 MiB, vs 194 MiB measured (91% — consistent,
small coalescing). At 3 projections/expert that is **~253 reads/token**.

Per-request fixed cost from the storage microbenchmark: 4 KB at 8 threads = 0.3 GB/s ⇒ 73k IOPS ⇒
~110 µs service latency per read at that concurrency. Single-thread 1 MB p50 = 0.67 ms, 4 MB = ~1.9
ms ⇒ a linear fit gives fixed *a* ≈ 0.10–0.26 ms and per-thread streaming ~1.9–2.2 GB/s. I use
**a = 0.10–0.25 ms** as an honest band; narrowing it is itself an experiment (below).

Device aggregate ceiling is 3.0–3.2 GB/s, so **one expert (2.65 MB) cannot arrive in less than ~0.83
ms no matter how many lanes it is split across.** This number dominates section 3.

## 1. Flash-level: io_uring, UFS features, polling, sub-read splitting

**io_uring from an app (Termux): blocked, demonstrated.** Android's seccomp filter is installed into
zygote and blocks `io_uring_setup` (arm64 syscall 425) for app processes; attempts SIGSYS/EPERM.
(Android Developers Blog, seccomp in Android O; Phoronix, Google restricting io_uring; litter#354 —
SIGSYS on syscall 425 on Pixel 9.)

**io_uring from `adb shell`: probably still blocked, but by SELinux, not seccomp.** AOSP documents
that the seccomp policy "is applied only to zygote spawned processes", so an adb-shell binary is
*not* seccomp-filtered. But AOSP sepolicy carries an `anon_inode` class and an `io_uring_use()`
macro granted explicitly per domain; I fetched `private/shell.te` from AOSP main and **it contains
no `io_uring`, `anon_inode` or `memfd` rule**, so ring creation should be default-denied (EACCES +
an avc denial in logcat). Also relevant: `neverallow all_untrusted_apps domain:anon_inode *`. (not
verified on this device)

**Even if it worked, the payoff is small.** io_uring removes syscall/submission overhead (~1–3
µs/read ⇒ 0.25–0.75 ms/token over 253 reads) and thread wakeups, not device latency.
`IORING_SETUP_IOPOLL` needs a block driver `->poll`, which UFS/SCSI does not implement (NVMe-only in
practice) — so NVMe-style polling is unavailable. The lanes are only ~35% utilised, i.e. submission
is not the constraint.

**UFS features.** HPB caches the L2P table in host RAM and is worth 50–70% on *4 KB* random reads
(HPB patch series cover letter, linux-scsi); at 0.88–2.65 MB the L2P lookup is amortised to nothing,
and it is a kernel/vendor feature with no userspace handle. MCQ (UFSHCI 4.0, in Linux since 6.2)
shortens completion-interrupt paths and steers completions to the submitting core (Linaro blog; LWN
910515); vendors published no numbers, and it is not reachable from userspace on an unrooted phone.
`posix_fadvise` is a no-op under O_DIRECT (FADV_RANDOM only zeroes readahead, which O_DIRECT already
bypasses). `ioprio_set` RT class needs CAP_SYS_ADMIN.

**Splitting one expert across 4 lanes: arithmetically dead.** 2.65 MB over 4 sub-reads would need 6
GB/s to beat the 3-read case; the device plateaus at 3.2 GB/s from 4 threads. Best case ~0.95 ms vs
~0.83 ms floor — no material gain.

**Status: demonstrated (app blocked) / theoretical (shell blocked); expected effect ≤0.5–1 ms/token
(<0.5%). Experiment that settles it:** a 30-line adb-shell binary calling `io_uring_setup(8,&p)` and
reporting errno + `logcat | grep avc`; and a pread latency-vs-size sweep at 0.25/0.5/0.88/1.76/2.65
MB × 1–8 threads to pin *a* and the per-thread slope, which every estimate below depends on.

## 2. Layout: expert-contiguous, aligned

**Demonstrated elsewhere.** llama.cpp discussion #27149 reports a relayout tool taking one expert's
footprint from 6,912 pages to 192 (**36×**), with ~2.2 MB/expert at Q4 and 2.97 GB/s sequential,
6.06 GB/s double-buffered on an NVMe host. Discussion #18758 measures a co-activation-ordered layout
cutting reads/token 1418 → 775 and cold decode I/O **2.23× faster**, and explicit slice reads
beating faults by 13–14% end-to-end. Prior art for the idea: Apple's row–column bundling stores the
i-th up-column with the i-th down-row to double chunk size (arXiv 2312.11514); Ripple reorders
neuron placement in flash for continuity and reports **up to 5.93×** I/O-latency improvement (arXiv
2410.19274); PowerInfer-2 bundles co-activated neurons into sequential blocks (arXiv 2406.06282);
EdgeMoE keeps experts in storage and fetches on activation (arXiv 2308.14352). Note the honest
caveat: Ripple's and PowerInfer-2's gains are **entangled with prediction/co-activation**, which is
closed here; what transfers is only the *contiguity* component.

**Arithmetic for this device.** Collapsing 253 reads → 84.5 reads removes 169 × *a* of lane-time =
**17–42 ms of lane occupancy, i.e. 4–11 ms of wall time across 4 lanes**. O_DIRECT padding is a
second-order bonus: GGUF's default tensor alignment is 32 B, so each of 253 reads pays up to 8 KB of
round-out ≈ 2 MB/token (~1%); 4 KB-aligning the relayout removes it.

**It does *not* fix head-of-line.** Today the first missing expert's three projections go out on
three lanes in parallel; fusing them into one request does not make 2.65 MB arrive sooner, and the
0.83 ms bandwidth floor binds either way. Claiming otherwise would be the error this repo's
CLAUDE.md §4 warns about.

**Status: demonstrated on other hosts, theoretical here; expected 4–11 ms/token (2–6%).
Experiment:** write a `--layout expert-contiguous` GGUF rewriter, re-run the pinned configuration,
and report stall-vs-bytes regression before/after — the fixed term should be unchanged and the
slope should improve.

## 3. Exact scheduling inside the token — is there any early-routing trick?

**No exact early routing exists for Qwen3-30B-A3B.** The Qwen3 technical report states Qwen3-MoE
**excludes shared experts** (unlike Qwen2.5-MoE), 128 experts, top-8 (arXiv 2505.09388). So there is
no always-resident shared expert to compute while routed experts stream. Pre-gated MoE (arXiv
2308.12066) obtains next-block routing one layer early, but only by **retraining a pre-gate
function** — it is an architecture change with an accuracy delta, i.e. lossy for an off-the-shelf
checkpoint. gpt-oss-20b (24 layers, 32 experts, top-4, arXiv 2508.10925) uses a standard
post-attention linear router; Qwen3-Next does have shared experts (HF model card), but that is a
different model, not a scheduling trick. **Report honestly: nothing exact exists at layer
granularity.**

**But there is exact slack *inside* the expert.** SwiGLU requires `gate` and `up` before the hidden
vector, and `down` only after. So `down`'s arrival can be deferred by one matvec — roughly ⅔ of the
expert's compute. This is exact, prediction-free, and it argues against fusing all three
projections: the right layout is **`gate|up` adjacent (one 1.76 MB read) and `down` separate (0.88
MB), issued at the same time but with `down` off the critical path**. Expected: removes the `down`
fraction (~1/3 of 2.65 MB ≈ 0.28 ms/layer at the 3.2 GB/s floor) from head-of-line on the first
missing expert of each layer — **up to ~13 ms/token** if head-of-line is fully exposed, far less if
it is already partly hidden. The measured fixed term is 11–27 ms, so this is the only identified
lever that attacks it directly.

**Status: untested (I found no source doing expert-internal split-phase scheduling for streaming
MoE — "not found", not "verified absent"); expected 0–13 ms/token. Experiment:** instrument
per-layer "router done → first expert usable" and "→ down needed" timestamps; the gain is bounded by
the measured gap between them.

## 4. The 13–34 ms cache-management term

**This is the largest identified lossless win, and the fix is to stop doing the work.** 194
MiB/token of freshly committed anonymous memory = **49,664 page faults/token at 4 KB**. At a
conservative 1.0–1.5 µs per anonymous fault (zero-fill + PTE + accounting) that is 50–75 ms/token —
more than the measured 13–34 ms, which tells you the current code already avoids some of it, and
equally that any residual decommit/recommit is expensive. `MADV_DONTNEED` additionally triggers TLB
shootdowns; on Snapdragon 888 `ptep_clear_flush` alone was 5.36% of CPU for single-process
page-out, and IPI latency is ~1–5 µs/core (LWN 928643).

The remedy is a **pre-committed, never-decommitted slot arena**: one `mmap(MAP_ANONYMOUS)` of the
full cache at startup, `MADV_POPULATE_WRITE` once (Linux ≥5.14; the reference benchmark shows 0.056
ms per 2 MiB memfd, ~0.028 ms/MiB, so ~110 ms for 4 GB, one time — madvise(2); LWN 846501), and an
LRU that overwrites slots in place with O_DIRECT. Steady-state cost then becomes zero faults, zero
zeroing, zero shootdowns. `--defer-evict` style batching only amortises the shootdowns; it cannot
remove the zero-fill, so it is strictly weaker than never decommitting. `MADV_FREE` is also strictly
weaker (lazy, but the page still gets re-zeroed if reclaimed). `process_madvise(MADV_COLD/PAGEOUT)`
is Android's own tool and is useful for the *opposite* purpose (section 5) — Google reports 15–30%
fewer LMKD kills from it (LPC 2019).

Second-order: if the kernel is a 16 KB-page build (Android 15+ supports `CONFIG_ARM64_16K_PAGES`;
source.android.com) or THP is `madvise`-enabled, `MADV_HUGEPAGE` on the arena cuts the one-time
populate 4×/512×. Whether this OnePlus 15R build uses 4 KB or 16 KB pages is **not verified** —
`getconf PAGESIZE` settles it.

**Status: theoretical, with strong mechanism evidence; expected 10–30 ms/token (5–16%) — the single
biggest item in this memo. Experiment:** arena allocator behind a flag; measure `perf stat -e
page-faults` (or `/proc/self/stat` min_flt delta) per token before/after and confirm the cache-mgmt
term collapses.

## 5. Holding 4+ GB for hours as an unprivileged process

Facts that constrain this: `android:largeHeap` affects only the ART heap and is irrelevant to native
`mmap`; native anonymous memory counts fully in PSS and is exactly what LMKD targets; OxygenOS is
independently documented as among the most aggressive background killers (SlashGear); LMKD is a
userspace daemon driven by PSI and `oom_score_adj` (source.android.com/docs/core/perf/lmkd).

What LMKD/zram treat most kindly, in order: (1) **clean file-backed pages** — reclaimable without a
kill and with no zram CPU cost; (2) anonymous pages marked `MADV_COLD`, which are reclaimed
preferentially over killing; (3) plain anonymous pages — worst, and Q4_0 weights are
near-incompressible so zram is pure CPU loss on them. This creates a genuine tension with §4: the
never-decommit arena is the most kill-prone shape of memory there is. The mitigation that preserves
both is **`mlockall(MCL_CURRENT)`/`mlock` on the arena** (needs RLIMIT_MEMLOCK headroom — `ulimit -l`
in adb shell, not verified) plus keeping the process in the `shell` domain, which is already why 6.7
GB was available. Note the measured 1.6–4.8 GB app-domain budget is bimodal, so **any app-packaged
product must size the cache from `ActivityManager.getMemoryInfo()` at runtime, not a constant** —
and a foreground service is necessary but not sufficient.

**Status: partly demonstrated (LMKD/zram mechanics), untested on this device; expected effect:
enables or forbids everything else rather than adding ms. Experiment:** a 6-hour soak holding a
locked 4 GB arena in the shell domain with periodic app switching, logging `/proc/self/status` and
LMKD kills.

## 6. Unconventional: accelerator-visible reads, GPU DMA, compressed experts

**O_DIRECT into a dma-buf/AHardwareBuffer: expected to fail.** O_DIRECT requires `get_user_pages` on
the destination; dma-buf mmap generally lacks struct-page backing, and the only known workaround is
udmabuf's experimental "quirk-mmap-page" mode (ikwzm/udmabuf). Separately, `/dev/dma_heap/*` access
is per-heap SELinux-gated since Android 12 (AOSP dma-buf heaps), and the Hexagon NPU session is
limited to ~3.5 GB of mapping, below the cache size. No evidence exists of any GPUDirect-Storage
equivalent on Adreno/Android — treat as unavailable (not verified absent).

**Compressing experts on flash: the evidence is against it.** ZipNN states plainly that GPTQ/AWQ
models still compress to 85–91%, but "**other quantized models using GGUF do not compress at all**"
(arXiv 2411.05239v2); its single-thread decompression is 1.4–2.5 GB/s on a Xeon. DFloat11 gets ~30%
by Huffman-coding **BF16 exponents** — a field Q4_0 does not have (arXiv 2504.11651). EntroLLM
reports ~1.5–2.0× on 4-bit/int8 but publishes no ARM decode throughput (arXiv 2505.02380) — treat
its ratio as unverified for Q4_0 specifically. The arithmetic: a Q4_0 block is an fp16 scale plus 32
near-uniform nibbles; a Gaussian source at 16 uniform levels has ~3.7–3.9 bits of entropy, so ≤8% on
the nibbles plus a little on the scales — call it ≤10%, i.e. ~19 MiB/token saved ≈ 3–4 ms, in
exchange for needing ~800 MB/s/core of entropy decode on four little cores that are also running the
I/O lanes. **Negative; do not pursue.**

**Status: theoretical negative on all three; expected effect ≈0 or negative. Experiment that settles
compression cheaply:** `zstd -1`, `zstd -19` and an FSE/rANS nibble coder on 200 MB of the actual
Q4_0 expert tensors, reporting ratio and single-core ARM decode MB/s. If the ratio is under ~1.15,
close it permanently.

## Ranked interventions

| # | Intervention | Evidence class | Expected (ms/token of 187) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-committed never-decommitted slot arena (+`MADV_POPULATE_WRITE`, +`MADV_HUGEPAGE` if available) — §4 | theoretical, strong mechanism | 10–30 (5–16%) |
| 2 | Split-phase expert read: `gate\|up` first, `down` deferred one matvec — §3 | untested, exact by construction | 0–13 |
| 3 | Expert-contiguous, 4 KB-aligned GGUF relayout — §2 | demonstrated elsewhere (36× pages, 2.23× cold I/O) | 4–11 |
| 4 | Per-lane batched submission, no thread wake per completion — §1 | theoretical | 2–5 |
| 5 | `mlock`ed arena in shell domain to sustain / grow the cache (bytes fall linearly with miss rate) — §5 | untested | indirect, potentially largest |

**Two caveats the caller should carry forward.** First, items 2–4 all divide a per-request or
per-layer constant whose value (*a* = 0.10–0.25 ms) is known only to a factor of 2.5 — the
latency-vs-size sweep in §1 should be run *before* any of them, or the estimates are noise. Second,
§1's own headline (io_uring) and §6 are negatives: the honest expected value of the flash-level and
compression directions on this device is approximately zero, and reporting them as such is the
finding.
