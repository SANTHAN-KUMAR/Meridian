# moe-phone — research position

**Status (2026-09-14): problem selection, levels L1–L3.** Nothing here is an on-device measurement.
The only computed result is the flash-read roofline `G-ROOF`, and it runs on **assumed** phone
parameters until gates G0/G1 measure the OnePlus 15R. Every number of ours lives in an artifact
under `results/<date>/` and is regenerated by a named script (`CLAUDE.md` §7.1); numbers quoted
below are **external literature values**, each with its source.

Read [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) first. Definitions live in [`ESTIMAND.md`](ESTIMAND.md).

## 0. The claim we are trying to earn — a method, validated by prediction

*Revised 2026-09-14 at the project owner's direction: a general method, not a race against one
system.*

> **A method for running any stock Mixture-of-Experts checkpoint beyond DRAM on a phone — no
> retraining, no distillation, no trained auxiliary networks. From a checkpoint's `config.json` and
> the phone's measured storage profile, the method predicts decode rate and energy per token and
> selects the configuration (expert cache, lookahead, gate-first sparsity, read layout, precision,
> processor placement). The prediction is validated on models it was not fitted to.**

Why a predictive method rather than a faster engine: a prediction registered before a measurement
is falsifiable, and it generalises — a reader can apply it to a model released after the paper.
The four earlier candidate directions become components of one pipeline:

| component | earlier option | role in the method |
|---|---|---|
| storage / locality / sparsity characterisation | B ("measure first") | the measured inputs of the predictor (G0–G3) |
| byte budget + roofline | — | the predictor itself (`gates/byte_budget.py`, then fitted engine overheads) |
| quantization → routing → reads | C | the precision term: bits change both bytes and routing |
| uncertainty under approximation | D | part of fidelity: an approximation must preserve calibration (ECE), not only KL |

Validation points, in order of importance:

1. **Held-out prediction.** Fit engine overheads on some models, predict decode rate for the others
   before running them, report prediction error.
2. **PowerInfer-2 as one data point**, not the thesis: at its reported budgets (7–19 GB), on its own
   released model (TurboSparse-Mixtral) run *unmodified* without its unreleased predictor, and on
   stock fine-grained MoEs.
3. **The scale frontier.** The largest total-parameter model the 15R serves at a decode rate derived
   from human reading speed (`ESTIMAND.md` §4), at pre-registered fidelity. Candidate models range
   from 7B to 235B total. No ≥60B-total model was found reported on an Android phone (search
   2026-09-14, §9).

## 1. What PowerInfer-2 actually claims — the baseline, sourced

From [arXiv 2406.06282 v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06282) (arXiv only; no venue found; **code
not released** — [SJTU-IPADS/PowerInfer#207](https://github.com/SJTU-IPADS/PowerInfer/issues)
unanswered as of 2026-09-14):

| item | value | location |
|---|---|---|
| devices | OnePlus 12 (24 GB, 19 GB usable, UFS 4.0); OnePlus Ace 2 (16 GB, 11 GB usable) | Table 3 |
| headline | TurboSparse-Mixtral-47B **11.68 tok/s at 19 GB**; **2.13 tok/s at 7 GB** | Fig. 10 (intermediate budgets: read from figure, Phase 0) |
| stock (SiLU) model | Mistral-7B **5.3 tok/s**, "2.4×" over their LLM-in-a-Flash reimplementation, 50% FFN offloaded | Table 6 |
| energy | **0.257 J/token** (model per Table 8 — to confirm) | Table 8 |
| quantization | INT4 per-channel, INT8 for outlier weights | §Implementation |
| memory at 7 GB budget | ~2.6 GB of it is **predictor weights** | §Evaluation |
| privileges | **root required** (`mlock`) | §Implementation |

The model: [TurboSparse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05955) replaces SwiGLU with dReLU and continues
pretraining for **150B tokens**. Weights are public
([PowerInfer/TurboSparse-Mixtral](https://huggingface.co/PowerInfer/TurboSparse-Mixtral), Apache-2.0,
safetensors); the Mixtral predictor is **not** released (PowerInfer issue #203).

**Reading of the baseline.** PowerInfer-2's speed comes from three things that are separable: a
retrained model with ~97% exact-zero FFN activations, trained predictors, and an I/O pipeline. Our
constraint removes the first two. Whether the third plus better system design can compensate is
the question — and `G-ROOF` already says it depends on the model family (§3).

## 2. The assumptions PowerInfer-2 rests on — each is a hypothesis we test

Each row is designed so **both outcomes are a primary result**, not a supporting one (§8).

| id | assumption in PowerInfer-2's design | our hypothesis | test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | useful flash savings need a model **retrained** to be activation-sparse | stock MoE experts are already sparse enough, training-free ([2605.08575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08575) reports up to 90% intra-expert sparsity without modification — GPU compute only, never I/O, never a phone) | G3: fidelity vs density on stock models |
| **A2** | sparsity must be **predicted** by trained networks (2.6 GB of a 7 GB budget) | a **gate-first** read — load gate rows, compute the gate exactly, then load only needed up/down rows — needs no predictor; on dReLU models (TurboSparse) it is **exact**, because a zero gate zeroes the neuron | G3, G5 |
| **A3** | bytes saved ≈ time saved | phone UFS loses several× per byte on small random reads (PowerInfer-2's own UFS measurements: ~3.5 GB/s at 512 KB vs 0.45–1 GB/s at 4 KB), so **read granularity, not bytes, bounds sparse streaming** | G1 + G4 |
| **A4** | expert/neuron locality is high enough to cache | measure it: hit rate vs cache size on real routing traces, against the no-locality floor | G2 |
| **A5** | the NPU should run decode's hot neurons | decode is memory-bound; CPU/GPU may match the NPU there ([HeteroLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14794); [2607.05475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05475)) — placement is measured, not assumed | G6 |
| **A6** | root is needed (`mlock`) | beyond-DRAM inference is possible without root, at a measurable cost | G0, G6 |

## 3. What the oracle already says (`G-ROOF`, assumed phone parameters)

`python moe-phone/gates/byte_budget.py …` (command in the script docstring) computes, per model,
the best decode rate any engine could reach if the only costs were flash reads of non-resident
expert weights and DRAM reads of everything else. It is an **upper bound**; every ignored cost
lowers it. Three qualitative results, which the measured gates must confirm or overturn:

1. **Coarse-grained MoE is the hard case, and it is PowerInfer-2's showcase.** Mixtral's experts
   are few and large, so a 12 GB cache holds a small fraction of them and each token touches
   several GB. Under the no-locality floor, the training-free bound for stock Mixtral is below
   PowerInfer-2's measured 7 GB point. **Prediction: a same-model, training-free win on stock
   Mixtral at phone budgets is not reachable;** the claim must be made on the PowerInfer-2 *model*
   (A2's exact gate-first path) or on fine-grained MoEs. If measurement agrees, that is itself a
   finding about *which architectures need retraining* (§8, F1).
2. **Fine-grained MoEs move the frontier far past 47B.** Models with many small experts (Qwen3-Next,
   gpt-oss) touch far fewer bytes per token, so the bound for 80B–120B-total models is in the range
   PowerInfer-2 reports for 47B — with no retraining.
3. **The granularity wall.** Rerunning with scattered reads charged at bulk bandwidth
   (`--tag bundled_ideal`) roughly doubles the sparse bounds. So the value of intra-expert sparsity
   is gated by read size: without a co-activation layout that turns neuron reads into large reads,
   most of the saved bytes are lost to UFS small-read inefficiency.

A fourth, unverified: TurboSparse-Mixtral's checkpoint holds ~5B more parameters than its
architecture accounts for (`resident_crosscheck_rel_diff` in the artifact) — consistent with merged
predictor weights. If confirmed from tensor names, a predictor-free engine reclaims that memory as
expert cache. Registered as stub S2.

## 4. Research questions

- **RQ1 (headline).** At PowerInfer-2's memory budgets, can a training-free engine match or beat its
  decode rate and J/token — (a) on TurboSparse-Mixtral with exact gate-first sparsity, (b) on stock
  fine-grained MoEs at Tier-A fidelity?
- **RQ2 (reads).** How many flash bytes per token, and how many flash *requests* per token, does each
  technique remove — expert caching with training-free lookahead ([Fate, 2502.12224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12224)),
  gate-first sparsity, co-activation layout, precision — and how much of each saving survives as time?
- **RQ3 (scale).** What is the largest model the 15R serves at the reading-speed threshold, and what
  single quantity predicts it? (Hypothesis: active bytes/token × (1 − hit rate) ÷ effective read
  bandwidth — i.e. `G-ROOF`'s structure, with measured inputs.)
- **RQ4 (limits).** Which wall binds first as model size grows — locality, granularity, prediction,
  OS memory, thermals, or compute — and is it fundamental or an engineering artifact?

## 5. Rules of the game (fixed before any result)

- **No gradient updates of any kind** to the model; no distillation; no trained predictors in the
  headline configuration (trained predictors may appear only as a labelled ablation).
- **Allowed**: unmodified published checkpoints; published post-training formats (Q4_0, Q8_0, native
  MXFP4); runtime decisions (what to read, when, where to compute). Sparsity thresholds are set on a
  generic calibration corpus **that never includes an evaluation task** (`CLAUDE.md` §6.4).
- **Two fidelity tiers, always reported separately** (`ESTIMAND.md` §3):
  **Tier E (exact)** — logits identical to a reference engine at the same weights and format.
  **Tier A (approximate)** — KL to the BF16 model within a pre-registered margin, plus task accuracy.
  "Without compromising quality" is claimed only at Tier E, or at Tier A within the margin.
- Every speed figure carries its memory budget, context length, warm/cold state, thermal state and
  run-to-run spread. No steady-state number from a cold device.

## 7. Experiment plan — cheapest kill first

| gate | question | where | cost | if it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G0** | 15R bring-up: NPU (HTP v81) visible to llama.cpp/QNN? RAM available to one app before LMK? root/unlock? Perfetto power rails? | 15R | 1 day | NPU dead → S25 fallback; no rails → battery-counter energy or Monsoon |
| **G1** | UFS read throughput vs request size (4 KB–4 MB), queue depth, direct vs buffered I/O, issuing core; energy per GB | 15R | 1 day | none — this *is* a result; replaces G-ROOF's assumed inputs |
| **G-ROOF v2** | rerun the oracle with G0/G1 measurements | laptop | minutes | a target below its bound is dropped **before** building |
| **G2** | routing traces: expert hit rate vs cache size (LRU, Belady-optimal), Fate-style lookahead accuracy, on stock models | Kaggle / rented H100 | 1–2 days | hit rate ≈ floor → locality wall (F4) |
| **G3** | fidelity vs density: gate-first sparsity on stock models (KL, flip rate, tasks); exact-zero fraction on TurboSparse | Kaggle / H100 | 2–3 days | fidelity collapses → F1 |
| **G4** | neuron co-activation statistics → bundle layout → request-size distribution → predicted flash time | laptop | 2 days | no layout recovers large reads → F2 |
| **G5** | engine, built incrementally on llama.cpp: expert cache + training-free lookahead → gate-first sparse reads → bundled layout → heterogeneous placement; each step ablated | laptop + 15R | weeks | each step's gain vs its G-ROOF prediction is reported, win or lose |
| **G6** | head-to-heads (RQ1), scale frontier (RQ3), energy, 30-min sustained thermal runs, root vs non-root | 15R (S25 replication) | 1–2 weeks | — |

Hardware: BF16 references for 30B+ models need ~60 GB+ → rented H100 for hours; smaller models on
Kaggle; the college H100 only if traces for 120B–235B references are required. The laptop RTX 4060
builds and runs the simulator.

## 8. Failure modes are results — the map

Each wall below is written as the paper it becomes if it binds. Ranked by expected strength.

| id | wall | if it binds, the paper is | strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | training-free sparsity fails fidelity on stock MoE (contradicting 2605.08575), or works on fine-grained but not coarse-grained MoE | **"When does an LLM need to be retrained to run from flash?"** — the sparsity–fidelity frontier across MoE granularity, reconciling 2605.08575 (MoE, sparse) with ActiveFlow's dense-model collapse. Answers whether PowerInfer-2's 150B-token retraining was necessary, per architecture | strong (ML venue) |
| **F2** | bytes saved do not become time saved | **"Read granularity, not bytes, bounds flash-streamed inference on phones"** — a measured UFS model + layout that recovers large reads for *stock* models (Ripple did this only for ReLU models) | strong (systems venue) |
| **F6** | without root, Android's memory manager (LMK, page cache, no `mlock`) caps beyond-DRAM inference | **"Beyond-DRAM inference as an unprivileged app"** — PowerInfer-2 required root; the unprivileged regime is the one real users are in and is uncharacterised | strong, practical |
| **F4** | routing has little locality at phone cache sizes | routing-locality characterisation of modern fine-grained MoE + what it implies for flash serving and for model design | medium |
| **F3** | neurons cannot be known before reading; gate-first serialises latency | predictability of intra-expert activation without training; latency hiding across layers | medium |
| **F5** | the NPU does not help decode | measured placement and energy on a 2025 SoC, correcting marketing-level assumptions | medium |
| **F7** | sustained runs throttle to a fraction of peak | thermally-aware scheduling for long generations | medium |
| F8 | static quantization flips routing and inflates reads | the quantization → routing → cache chain (earlier candidate; now one term, not the thesis) | supporting |
